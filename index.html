Finding 1 — There Is Nowhere For A Roofer To Apply
Read from the live site
Careers or jobs page none Application form none Contact form 1 — for customers Phone 01244 456882 and 07853 101658, both tappable

A roofer in Manchester looking for a job right now cannot apply to you. The only route is to ring the same number a customer rings, during the working day, and ask.

Good roofers are almost always already employed. They look on their phone, between jobs, in the afternoon — and they apply to whoever makes it take thirty seconds. If applying means an awkward phone call to a stranger while they are on somebody else's roof, most simply do not.

That is the whole finding. You are competing for scarce people with no way for them to reach you.

Finding 2 — Forty To Fifty Jobs A Month Is Your Best Recruitment Advert
And it appears nowhere

The single thing a good roofer wants to hear is that the work will not run out. Feast and famine is the defining problem of the trade — being sent home when it rains, or when the schedule falls apart, or when the boss has not sold anything that fortnight.

You do forty to fifty jobs a month. Most of your competitors cannot say that, and it is worth more in a job advert than almost anything except the pay.

What most roofing job ads say "competitive rates, immediate start" What you could say "we do 40-50 jobs a month. The work is already sold. You will not be sent home."

The second one is true and specific. The first is what everybody writes.

Finding 3 — Publish The Pay
The biggest single lever on how many people apply

Job adverts that state a pay range consistently attract far more applications than those saying “competitive”. It also does something more useful: it filters out everybody who was never going to accept your number, before they take up your time.

Most contractors resist this, usually because they do not want existing staff comparing. That is a fair concern and it is worth thinking about before the campaign runs rather than after — but a range rather than a single figure usually handles it.

What we would ask on the call: what you pay now, what you would pay for somebody good, and whether there is a difference. There usually is, and it is the difference that gets the applications.

Finding 4 — Nothing Is Measured, Which Matters For Hiring Too
Searched the full page source
Google Analytics not present Google Tag Manager not present Google Ads tag not present Meta pixel not present Call tracking not present

People assume tracking is only for customer advertising. It is not. A recruitment campaign has exactly the same shape — spend at the top, applications in the middle, hires at the bottom — and without measurement you cannot tell what an applicant costs you, let alone a hire.

The number that matters is cost per hire. Compare it against what a recruitment agency charges — typically a percentage of first-year salary — and you will usually find the campaign is a fraction of it. But only if it is being counted.

Finding 5 — And When The Crew Exists
So the order is clear

Your site does the customer job reasonably well already — a working form, both numbers tappable, free surveys offered, and the coverage area named clearly.

What it is missing on the customer side is tracking, a visible rating and review count, and pages for the individual towns. All of that is worth doing — after you have the people, because more enquiries into a business already running at capacity produces slower responses and unhappier customers, not growth.

We would rather sell you the second thing second.

What Is Genuinely Good
This is a business with a capacity problem, not a marketing problem
  • Forty to fifty jobs a month. Most roofers we audit would trade places with you immediately.
  • A working contact form and both a landline and a mobile, both tappable. More than most trade sites manage.
  • Your title tag is properly written — “Roofers in Manchester & Cheshire”. Trade and area, in the right order.
  • Free initial roof surveys offered clearly — a strong, no-margin offer.
  • All aspects of roofing and roofline, pitched and flat, repairs and new. A broad service list that supports a bigger crew.
  • Email on your own domain.
  • A limited company with a landline and a real address area — the signals a serious applicant looks for as much as a customer does.

Fix First
  • A careers page with an application form that works on a phone in under a minute
  • Decide on a pay range you are willing to publish — it multiplies applications
  • Lead with the volume — forty to fifty jobs a month is steady work, and roofers want steady work
  • Work out what a hire is worth, so the campaign has a number to beat
Build Next
  • Recruitment ads on Meta, aimed at roofers within a sensible drive of Manchester
  • A paid trial day rather than an interview — a day on a roof tells you what an hour cannot
  • Track applications like leads, so cost per hire is a number rather than a feeling
  • Then the customer-side work — tracking, reviews and town pages, once the crew exists

Everybody else will offer you more jobs. You have forty to fifty a month already.

The campaign that actually helps is the one that finds you three or four roofers.
